BOUNDED GAPS BETWEEN PRIMES
JULIA STADLMANN
Abstract.Polymath 8b [5] proved thatH 1= lim inf n→∞ (pn+1−pn)≤246. In this paper we show how
the Bombieri-Vinogradov theorem can be combined with newer equidistribution estimates for smooth
moduli to obtain the improved boundH 1≤240.
1.Introduction
In this paper we studyH 1= lim inf n→∞(pn+1−pn), the smallest gap between consecutive primes
which appears infinitely often. The twin prime conjecture suggests thatH 1= 2, but only since Zhang’s
breakthrough [7] in 2013 is it known thatH 1is finite. Zhang’s boundH 1≤70000000 has since been
improved several times. In particular, Maynard [3] showed thatH 1≤600, and the Polymath project [5]
improved this to the current best known boundH 1≤246. A core ingredient of Zhang’s result were
his newly proved equidistribution estimates for primes in arithmetic progressions with smooth moduli.
However, Maynard’s new sieve weights later allowed for the use of the older and simpler Bombieri-
Vinogradov theorem [3]. Polymath’s boundH 1≤246 also only uses the Bombieri-Vinogradov theorem
and no newer equidistribution estimates [5].
More generally, one may considerH m= lim inf n→∞(pn+m−pn), the gap betweenm+ 1 consecutive
primes. Maynard [3] first showed thatH mis bounded, giving estimateH m≪m3exp(4m), and Tao
obtained a similar bound around the same time. The Polymath project [5] improved this bound toH m≪
exp((4−28
157)m). Baker and Irving [1] first introduced Harman’s sieve into this problem, givingH m≪
exp(3.815m), which we improved toH m≪exp(3.8075m) [6]. Concrete upper bounds forH 2, . . . , H 5are
also known, see [5] and [6]. The best known upper bounds onH mform≥2 all use equidistribution
estimates of Zhang type.
Equidistribution estimates of Zhang type give an exponent of distribution larger than1
2, improving on
the exponent in the Bombieri-Vinogradov theorem, but only apply to a limited choice of moduli, likexδ-
smooth numbers. When applying the GPY method with Maynard-Tao sieve weights, a larger exponent
of distribution corresponds to a bigger function support in the relevant optimization problem, whereas a
restricted choice of moduli also restricts the support. In Polymath’s approach to proving upper bounds on
Hm[5], the gains from having a larger exponent of distribution outweigh the losses from being restricted
toxδ-smooth numbers whenm≥2, but not whenm= 1.
The aim of this paper is to combine the function support given by the Bombieri-Vinogradov theorem
with that of newer equidistribution estimates, so that we may benefit from the advantages of both, and
improve the bound onH 1. We prove the following:
Theorem 1.The quantityH 1= lim inf n→∞(pn+1−pn)satisfiesH m≤240.
The value 246 corresponds to the length of the shortest admissible 50-tuple, while 240 is the length of
the shortest admissible 49-tuple. The boundH 1≤240 is therefore the smallest possible improvement.
However, while Polymath [5] used symmetric polynomials of degree≤27 to achieveH 1≤246, we only
needed polynomials of degree≤21 to getH 1≤240. As such, we believe that our method could achieve
bigger improvements, but due to limited resources and limited time, we have decided to present this
modest improvement as a proof of concept.

method could be used to get a better bound onH 1.

1.2.Structure of the paper.In Section 2 we carry out the key steps of the GPY method with
Maynard-Tao sieve weights and Polymath’s epsilon-enlargement trick. Our arguments follow Polymath’s
approach [5], but many adjustments need to be made to deal with a more complicated function support
and account for the use of Harman’s sieve.
In Section 3 we record the equidistribution estimates used in our proof. Although the relevant estimates
are closely based on [4] and [6], the moduli restrictions in these papers are too strong, and we need to
prove relaxed conditions on the moduli.
The function support used in Section 2 is quite general and involves many different parameters. Dependent
on these parameters, the equidistribution estimates of Section 3 may or may not be good enough to cover
all relevant moduli. In Section 4 we show how to calculate good parameters for which the available
equidistribution estimates work well enough.
In Section 5 we briefly discuss how integrals over the chosen support can be expressed in terms of matrix
multiplication and recursion.
In Section 6 we combine the results of the previous sections to complete the proof of Theorem 1.
