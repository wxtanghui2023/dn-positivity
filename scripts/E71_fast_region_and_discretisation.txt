READING
  If |S_fast| carries the size while |I| is tiny, then the size is not in the oscillatory integral at
  all but in the discretisation, that is in the difference between summing over the zeros and integrating
  against the smooth density.  That difference is, by Poisson summation, a prime-side object, which is
  where the other routes also end.  The bound column shows what stationary phase gives for the integral,
  for comparison with the measured |I|.

  CAVEAT ON THIS RUN, recorded so the numbers are not over-read. The |I| column is NOT reliable:
  the integrand oscillates with phase n*theta, which changes by about a radian per grid step in the
  fast region, so the trapezoidal rule under-samples and the reported values are quadrature error
  rather than the integral. A proper evaluation needs a stationary-phase or analytic treatment, or
  quadrature on a phase-adapted grid; neither was done here. The valid conclusions from this run are
  only the two sum columns: the slow-region sum is small and the fast-region sum carries the size,
  the latter being close to tautological since the fast region holds most of the zeros.
