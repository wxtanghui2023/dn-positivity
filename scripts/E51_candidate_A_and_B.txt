READING
  Candidate A dies on concentration: the prime-side weight inherits the phase's spread, and
  decomposing it needs about the cube of the height in terms, so the new machinery offers nothing
  beyond the methods already tried and found short.  Candidate B survives the first test: on the
  project's own two million zeros the requirement holds at every sampled index across the whole
  quadratic range, with fluctuations a few times the random-phase scale, so an averaged theorem is
  consistent with the data and its input -- a second moment in n of the zero-side sum -- is of a
  type that is unconditionally available.  The price is that the theorem would hold for almost
  every index rather than every index.
