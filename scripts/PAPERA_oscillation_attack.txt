------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  *** CORRECTION TO MY OWN TEXT ABOVE ***
  The offsets printed are the distance to the nearest TABULATED ZERO, which is NOT
  the point of this test and is misleading. Truncation is allowed at ANY height T,
  not only at a zero. Since dtheta/dT = -4/(4T^2+1), moving T by delta changes the
  phase n*theta by about 4n*delta/T^2; requiring n*theta to hit a zero of cosine
  therefore costs only delta ~ T^2/n. For n = T0^2 that is delta = O(1), i.e. an
  adjustment of ORDER ONE in a height of order 10^6, and relative error T^2/n / T0
  which is at most T0^{-0.5} for the whole range n <= T0^2.

  *** CONSEQUENCE (the key positive finding) ***
  Because verification at height T0 implies verification at every smaller height,
  we may RESET the height to the phase-zero value T0' just below T0, losing only a
  relative error of order T0^{-0.5} in the range, and thereby kill the boundary term
  EXACTLY. This is precisely the NS pattern of CONTROLLED TERMINATION: choose the
  stopping point so that the unwanted term vanishes identically rather than bounding it.
