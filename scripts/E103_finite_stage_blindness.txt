E103 - finite-stage prime data is blind to the zeros
==============================================================================
box: sigma in [0.02, 0.98],  t in [0.5, 30.0]

zeros of zeta inside the box               : 3
first five ordinates                       : 14.1347, 21.0220, 25.0109, 30.4249, 32.9351

truncated zeta series D_N(s) = sum_{n<=N} n^{-s}: zeros inside the SAME box
       N    #zeros(D_N)    ratio to zeta
      10              6             2.00
      20              9             3.00
      50             14             4.67
     100             19             6.33

Reading: the count grows with the truncation and does not settle at the zeta count.
The truncated sums do not converge to zeta inside the strip (they diverge there),
so their zero sets cannot converge to zeta's zeros either.  Combined with Lemma A
(finite Euler products have no zeros in the strip at all) this says: the zeros are
created by the limit, and the limits that see them are not the naive finite ones.
==============================================================================
Boundary: this is not a theorem that analytic continuation is the UNIQUE limit
procedure; it only excludes the naive finite-stage limits and records that any
successful procedure must be limit-seeing.
