PART 2 -- the amplitude test: can any positivity-type gap statement see a finite off-line set?
   contribution of an off-line pair to psi(x+h)-psi(x) with h = sqrt(x):
        sum_rho [ (x+h)^rho - x^rho ] / rho   for the pair only
      x          |pair contribution|        sqrt(x)          ratio
      1e6        4.231371576                1000.0           4.231372e-03
      1e12       15.91202447                1000000.0        1.591202e-05
      1e24       251.1926242                1.0e+12          2.511926e-10
      1e48       63095.73587                1.0e+24          6.309574e-20
   (beyond 1e48 the subtraction of two nearly equal huge terms exceeds the working precision of
    forty digits; the decay law itself is x^{delta-1/2}, verified over four decades above)
   => the ratio decays like x^{delta-1/2} = x^{-0.4}: a finite off-line set is invisible at the
      square-root scale.  So a statement of the form 'the interval is nonempty' cannot separate
      a configuration with finitely many off-line zeros from one with none.

   what would be needed instead: coherence among many modes.  For a distance delta from the

   => for delta >= 1/4 the requirement is satisfiable in principle by a dense off-line
      spectrum, but NOT by any finite one.  Under the finiteness hypothesis the route is closed;
      without it, everything hinges on whether a single off-line zero forces a densely growing
      off-line population -- a question on which no theorem is known.

READING
  Part 1 confirms that the finite exponential sum has the expected recurrence and rank structure,
  and adds one constraint that follows from the modulus pairing: the two-sided sequence is even.
  Part 2 shows why no gap-type positivity statement can decide the question under the finiteness
  hypothesis: one off-line pair contributes at the square-root scale a quantity smaller by the
  factor x^{1/2-delta}, so it is invisible there, and the collective requirement is unsatisfiable
  by finitely many modes.  The route therefore reduces either to Li/Weil positivity (if one goes
  through the explicit formula) or to an open question about off-line density (if one does not).
