The paper is attached (5 pages). It concerns the Li criterion (RH ⟺ λ_n ≥ 0 for all n).
Its content is deliberately modest and elementary:

  Assume the zeros of ζ below a height T have been rigorously verified to lie on the
  critical line (interval arithmetic). Then λ_n ≥ 0 for every n with 2 ≤ n ≤ 2T − O(1).
  For T = 3.000175·10¹² this gives n up to ≈ 6.00035·10¹², about 6·10⁷ times the range
  reached by direct computation of λ_n in the literature (n ≤ 10⁵).

The proof is elementary and uses four ingredients: (i) on-line zeros contribute
1 − cos(nθ_γ) ≥ 0, where θ_γ = arg(1−1/ρ); (ii) on the window γ ∈ [n/2, 2n] each such term
is at least 1 − cos(1/2 − 1/(96n²)) (from the expansion θ_γ = 1/γ − 1/(12γ³) + …);
(iii) the classical explicit zero-counting bound supplies enough zeros in that window;
(iv) zeros above T contribute at worst −n·B_T with an explicitly computable B_T.

To be explicit about scope: this is a subset result, not a proof of RH, and I do not claim
novelty for converting a verified height into a range for λ_n — that conversion is a
folklore consequence of |1−1/ρ| = 1 on the line and > 1 off it. What the note provides is an
explicit, elementary and numerically calibrated form of the resulting range (with the
calibration λ_1 = 0.02309570897… reproduced to 8·10⁻⁵).

I would be very grateful for an endorsement if you find the paper suitable, and equally
grateful for any correction — if the range or the argument is already known, please tell me
and I will withdraw it immediately.
